01 / The gap

The industry's blind spot has a size.

Four documented populations, all large, all commercially underserved. These are the numbers we designed against.

  • 15.3M
    Americans with hyperhidrosis — 4.8% of the population. 70% report severe sweating in at least one body area; only 51% have ever raised it with a clinician.
    Doolittle et al., 2016 · International Hyperhidrosis Society
  • 67%
    of US women wear a size 14 or above — the majority of the market, historically underrepresented in mainstream retail.
    market.us, 2025 · Grand View Research, 2026
  • 34%
    of people with limited mobility dislike trouser pockets entirely — bulky, chafing, or wrong position when seated. 36% prefer front hip pockets.
    Neuromuscular Disorders, 2024
  • $119.4B
    plus-size clothing market value in 2024, growing faster than the broader women's apparel market.
    market.us, 2025

Sensory-friendly clothing is easy to find for children and nearly impossible to find for adults. No brand designs neurodivergent adult formalwear. That is where we start.

Sense-ational You, the closest direct competitor, sells primarily to children — its adult line is hoodies and shoes, $50–80. Mainstream comfort brands like UNIQLO cover some sensory needs by accident. Business and formal attire for neurodivergent adults is unaddressed.

02 / Research

What the research found

Six lines of inquiry, each ending in a decision about what to build.

Research findings and the design decision each one produced.
Research What it discovered HOZ takeaway
Waistbands & seated fit Sitting in a wheelchair puts sustained pressure on the waistband and hips; standard trousers sit too low at the back.1,2 Seated-first patterning is a fixable design problem, not a comfort preference.
Pockets 34% of people with limited mobility want no trouser pockets at all; 36% want front hip pockets only.1 Pocket configuration becomes a product variable, not a default.
Fastenings Nearly all respondents could manage an elasticated waist; very few could manage side-fastening trousers.2 Closure type is the single highest-leverage accessibility decision in a trouser.
Sensory adult sizing Smooth-seam, tagless, soft-fabric clothing is widely available for children and scarce in adult sizing. Stiff suits and collars are named as mentally taxing.3,4 Adult formalwear is the underserved wedge.
Decision fatigue Larger choice sets increase regret mainly because people feel rushed. When people believed they had enough time, set size stopped predicting regret.5 A guided quiz removes the time pressure, not just the options.
Synthetic fabric risk Petroleum feedstock is why synthetics undercut natural fibers. Washing sheds microplastic fibers; acrylic and nylon carry endocrine-disrupting chemicals.6,7,8 Material honesty is a health claim, not a sustainability claim.
03 / Principles

What are the design principles?

The process gives us concrete steps of design; principles are the guiding pillars of how we make decisions during the process.

01

Principle 01. Functionality

Does it perform correctly and reliably, in the conditions the wearer actually lives in?

02

Principle 02. Safety

Safety is first. Protect people, property and systems — including from the materials themselves.

03

Principle 03. Sustainability

Minimize environmental impact; design for efficient manufacturing.

04

Principle 04. Cost-effectiveness

Efficient use of resources. How do we match performance to a real budget?

05

Principle 05. Aesthetics & usability

Beautiful and intuitive. Disabled people deserve to want their clothes, not just tolerate them.

06

Principle 06. Scalability & manufacturing

Can it be built and repeated, efficiently, time after time?

07

Principle 07. Ethics & social responsibility

Design with positive impact in mind.

04 / System

How a signal becomes a garment

  1. 01

    Stage 01. Signal

    What people actually can't wear, read across communities. Fit notes, refusals and workarounds are collected as data, not anecdotes.

  2. 02

    Stage 02. Forecast

    Need converted to unit plans. Demand is sized before a pattern is cut, so a small run is priced honestly.

  3. 03

    Stage 03. Produce

    Vetted makers, matched by capability. Capability vetting covers seam finish, closure hardware and small-run tolerance.

  4. 04

    Stage 04. Return

    Value redistributed, not just diverted. The return path is designed with the garment, and it pays someone.

Every stage writes to the same record, so a fit note from one wearer can change what the next run is cut from.
